Micron closed on Thursday at $931.24. In its last fiscal year it earned $74.33 a share, so the stock trades at about 12.5 times trailing earnings. For its current quarter it has guided to $37.84 a share; four quarters at that pace would be about $151, or roughly six times the share price. That is my arithmetic on the guidance, not a forecast.

Look at what that multiple buys. Revenue in fiscal 2026 was $133.2 billion, against $37.4 billion the year before. The gross margin in the last quarter was 86.8%. Few companies of any kind earn margins like that, and fewer still at this size: on its 1.147 billion diluted shares, Micron is worth about $1.07 trillion.

So the question I keep hearing is a fair one. How long until the market prices Micron like the business it has become?

I think the question has the direction wrong. In memory, the gap between a low multiple and a great business usually closes, but not by the price rising to meet the earnings. It closes by the earnings falling to meet the price. Micron's own filings show it twice.

## 2018: the cheapest moment was the top

Micron's fiscal 2018 ended on August 30, 2018. It had earned $11.51 a share on a gross margin of 58.9%, records at the time. The stock closed that day at $52.76: about 4.6 times earnings. Anyone looking at that multiple would have asked the same question people ask today.

The answer came a year later. In fiscal 2019 revenue fell from $30.4 billion to $23.4 billion and earnings to $5.51 a share, less than half. The stock, on August 29, 2019, closed at $44.67, down 15%. The multiple rose to about 8.1 times. The market had not caught up with Micron. Micron had come down to the market.

## 2022: same lesson, harder

Fiscal 2022 ended on September 1, 2022, with earnings of $7.75 a share and a gross margin of 45.2%. The stock closed at $57.31, about 7.4 times. In fiscal 2023 revenue halved, from $30.8 billion to $15.5 billion, the gross margin went to minus 9.1%, and Micron lost $5.34 a share. On August 31, 2023, the stock closed at $69.94, 22% higher than a year before.

Read those two years together. Earnings fell by half, then from a profit to a loss. The stock fell 15% in the first case and rose 22% in the second. The low multiple at the top is what let the shares get through the bottom. It was not the market missing the profit. It was the market declining to pay for a profit it did not expect to last.

That is my answer to "how long". If the past is a guide, the next record is not what changes the multiple. The market already priced the records as temporary. The re-rating in this industry tends to come in the downturn, when earnings drop and the price drops less.

## Why this time could be different

The strongest objection is that this is not 2018. The margin is not 59% but 87%. Revenue is more than four times the 2018 peak. And as we wrote [on Wednesday](/opinion/article/?slug=asml-sells-the-tools-that-keep-micron-cheap), Micron has signed multi-year contracts with customers, many with price floors and cash deposits, built to make its profits less like a spot price and more like a queue.

I take that seriously. If those contracts hold when prices fall, Micron's earnings would drop less than in 2019 or 2023, and a market that has been burned twice would have reason to pay more for them.

But the size of the margin cuts both ways. An 87% gross margin is not only a sign of a great business. It is a signal to every competitor that adding capacity pays, and Micron itself told investors on September 30 that it will spend more in fiscal 2027 than it had planned, mostly on new cleanroom space. The higher the peak, the more supply it calls forth.

## What would change my mind

I would stop thinking of the low multiple as the market's memory if it started to rise while earnings were still rising, a re-rating from the top rather than from the bottom. In the two cycles above, it had none. Or if a quarter came in which memory prices fell and Micron's profits held up far better than in 2019 and 2023. Either would mean the market had decided that memory is no longer a cycle. Until then, I read the six times as the market's view of how long this lasts, not as a mistake waiting to be corrected.
