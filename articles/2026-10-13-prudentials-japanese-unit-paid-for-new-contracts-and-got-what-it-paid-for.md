On October 8 Prudential Financial filed two releases from its life insurer in Japan, Prudential Life Insurance Co. One publishes the findings of a Special Investigation Committee into what the company calls "inappropriate monetary conduct" by its salespeople. The other says what that means: soliciting customers to invest or offering them profit-making schemes in exchange for money, borrowing money from customers, and other breaches of the rules on money.

The count is 146 current and former sales employees. Of them, 53 were dismissed or told to resign, 68 were suspended and 25 received lesser measures. The company has approximately 4,000 sales employees. The cases announced in January involved 498 people and claims of 3.08 billion yen. Beyond those, by September 16 an independent reimbursement committee had reviewed 848 further inquiries about Prudential Life and its sister company Gibraltar Life, and found 329 eligible for reimbursement, for claims of 2.57 billion yen.

The company does not blame bad apples. It says the misconduct "was not limited to issues involving individual sales employees, but was a structural problem arising from its management framework and business model."

I think that sentence is right. The proof is in the list of remedies.

## Read the remedy as a confession

When a company says what it will change, it tells you what was there before. Three changes stand out.

Pay for after-sales service will go "from less than 5% of total compensation to approximately 20%". So until now, more than 95 percent of what a salesperson earned had nothing to do with serving the customer they already had.

The commission on a new contract, paid until now over four years, will be paid over seven.

And the managers. Under the old system, the release says, "the number of new hires was directly linked to compensation", and managers were judged mainly on the new contracts their people brought in. That link is being removed, base salary will rise to about 30 percent of a manager's pay, and managers will "in principle, no longer be permitted to sell insurance themselves."

Put those together. A salesperson whose income depends on the next contract. A customer who trusts that one person, not the company. A manager paid to recruit and to sell, not to watch. The committee found the relationships between salespeople and customers so "individualized and closed" that head office could not see into them, and that many cases went on "repeatedly over extended periods" without being detected.

Nobody's pay plan asked them to borrow from a customer. But the plan built the room in which it could happen, and paid no one to open the door.

## The repair is real. The accounting for it is not finished

I do not think the response is cosmetic. New business sales are voluntarily suspended, and so is recruitment. The 139 agencies are to be cut to between 40 and 50. Since October 7, customer meetings are recorded, with the customer's consent, and summarized by software. Head office will place its own quality staff in every agency. This is a company rebuilding the way it sells.

The thin part is the responsibility at the top. The president and two other executives will return 30 percent of their monthly pay for three months. By my arithmetic that is less than one month's pay each.

To be fair to them, they are the new management, in place since February 2026, and the release calls the gesture "one aspect of management responsibility". What it will do about former management is still to be decided, after advice from outside experts and votes of the boards.

That is the decision I would judge the whole exercise by. The company's own diagnosis is that management "remained bound by past successes, regarded the existing business model as absolute", and that, "despite having opportunities to recognize the structural nature of these issues, it failed to translate that recognition into fundamental reform." If the model was the cause, the people who kept the model are part of the cause. So far the discipline has fallen on 146 salespeople.

## The objection

The strongest one is that this model was the business. The committee says it "relied excessively on individuals' personal networks and relationships of trust". Those relationships are also why customers bought. A salesperson with a guaranteed minimum, a recorder on the table and a supervisor from head office may be a safer one who sells less.

I accept that, and I think it is the price. Sales that depend on nobody watching were worth less than they looked.

## What to watch

Three things. When, and on what terms, new sales resume: the company says it will "carefully determine whether and when". What the boards decide about former management. And a line that is easy to miss: the report also refers to "suspected inappropriate conduct in insurance solicitation", which the company is now examining in a self-review. That is no longer about money changing hands on the side. It is about the product itself.

What would change my mind is evidence that the pay plan was not the cause: for instance, the same conduct at the same rate once the new plan starts in 2027. I do not expect it. People tend to do what they are paid to do, and stop doing what nobody pays for.
