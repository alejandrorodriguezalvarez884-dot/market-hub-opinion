On Tuesday Digital Realty filed two announcements about borrowing. It priced €1 billion of notes due 2036 with a coupon of 5.125%, sold at 99.289% of face value. And it closed CHF510 million of Swiss franc bonds in three pieces: CHF225 million at 1.6803% due 2029, CHF185 million at 2.0600% due 2032 and CHF100 million at 2.4150% due 2036.

Now open the company's last quarterly report. At June 30 its debt came to $18.8 billion, at a weighted-average interest rate of 2.99%. The unsecured senior notes, $16.0 billion of the total, cost 2.60%.

A company whose notes cost 2.60% has just paid 5.125% for ten-year euros. I think that is the most important fact about Digital Realty's finances, and it gets far less attention than its leasing.

## A ladder of coupons

The quarterly report lists every note, and the list reads like a history of interest rates. The euro notes due in 2031 pay 0.625% and 1.250%. Those due in 2032 pay 1.000% and 1.375%. Then the newer ones: 3.750% and 3.875% on the notes due from 2033 to 2035, and 4.250% on the note due 2037, issued last November. Now 5.125%.

The francs tell the same story on a smaller scale. CHF275 million of notes paying 0.200% fall due on December 15, 2026. The new three-year franc bond pays 1.6803%. On CHF275 million, that is the difference between CHF0.55 million of interest a year and about CHF4.6 million.

The company has already said this is happening. Its interest expense in the first half of 2026 was $230.3 million, up $22.5 million on a year earlier, "driven primarily by" the euro notes it issued in June and November of 2025.

## The calendar

At June 30 the debt had a weighted average term to initial maturity of approximately 4.4 years. The maturity table is more exact: $825 million in the rest of 2026, $2.16 billion in 2027, $2.53 billion in 2028 and $3.60 billion in 2029. That is $9.1 billion, almost half of everything Digital Realty owes, by the end of 2029.

Not all of it is cheap. The schedule includes $900 million of notes at 5.550% due in 2028, and the secured debt costs 7.49% on average. But it also includes €500 million at 1.125% due in April 2028, which at Tuesday's euro coupon would cost €25.6 million a year in place of €5.6 million. It includes francs at 0.200% and 0.550%, and $1.15 billion of exchangeable notes at 1.875%.

A rule of thumb, which is my arithmetic and not a forecast of the company's: every $1 billion that moves from 2.60% to 5.125% adds about $25 million a year of interest.

That is why I call the old debt an asset. It does not appear on the balance sheet as one. But the right to owe money at 2.60% when lenders now ask 5% is worth real cash every year. And unlike a building, it has an expiry date printed on it.

## Why not borrow it all in francs

There is an obvious objection in Tuesday's own filings. On the same day, for the same final year and with the same parent guarantee, Digital Realty paid 2.4150% in francs and 5.125% in euros. Why not take the francs?

Because a coupon is the price of a currency as much as of a borrower. The company says it borrows "primarily" in "the functional currencies of the countries where we invest", and 62.9% of its debt is in euros because that is where much of its property and its rent are. Paying for euro buildings with franc debt trades a known interest cost for an unknown exchange rate. The franc bond is roughly half the size of the euro one for a reason. Low coupons are still on sale. They are in another currency.

## The stronger objection

The better answer to my worry is rent. Digital Realty builds and leases data centers in the middle of a building boom. The Federal Reserve's minutes, published Wednesday, record officials saying that "the scale and pace of the AI buildout had continued to surprise to the upside." If leases renew at prices that rise faster than coupons, the cost of debt is a detail.

Two more things soften the blow. The average moves slowly, since no single year brings even a fifth of the debt due. And part of the interest on money spent on construction is capitalized, added to the cost of the building and kept out of the interest line. The report says exactly that offset some of the first half's increase.

I accept all of it. But it changes what kind of company this is. With money at 0.625%, almost any rent on a full building left a margin. At 5.125%, the rent has to do the work alone.

## What I am watching

The weighted-average rate was 2.90% at the end of 2025 and 2.99% six months later. The next quarterly report will show it after Tuesday's borrowing. The December 15 franc maturity is the first small test, and 2027 the first large one.

What would change my mind is evidence that rents on renewed leases are outrunning that figure. Until I see it, I read 2.99% as a photograph of the past. The price of the present is the one on Tuesday's filings.
