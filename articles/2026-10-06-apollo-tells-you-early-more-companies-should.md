Apollo Global Management will publish its third-quarter results on November 3. On October 5, four weeks ahead of that, it filed a notice with one number from them: its estimate of alternative net investment income for the quarter, about $375 million before tax.

The company did not have to do this. I think it is the right thing to do, and that the practice should be more common than it is. I also think the way the number is presented flatters it, and the two points belong in the same article.

## Why this line, and why now

Alternative net investment income is what Apollo's retirement business, Athene, earns on the part of its portfolio held in alternative investments rather than in ordinary bonds. The filing explains where it sits: it is part of what the company calls Spread Related Earnings, which is in turn part of the income of its Retirement Services segment.

It is also the kind of line that moves around. Returns on alternative investments are not a steady coupon. A quarter can come in well above or well below what anybody assumed, and when it does, it can swing the result of the whole segment.

So the choice is between two ways of delivering the same fact. The company can keep it until results day, when it lands together with everything else and the market has minutes to work out how much of the surprise is this one line. Or it can publish it when the quarter has closed and the estimate is ready, and let results day be about the rest.

Apollo chose the second. The surprise, if there was one, is now a month old by the time the full results arrive.

## The honest part

The filing is careful about what it is. The figure is an estimate. The closing procedures for the quarter are not finished, and the actual result may differ materially. The auditors have neither audited nor reviewed it. It is no substitute for full financial statements.

That is the right amount of caution, stated where a reader will see it. A preliminary number with those words attached is more useful than no number, and a company that does this regularly gives up the ability to choose its moments. That matters. An early number published only in good quarters would be advertising. The value of the habit lies in doing it in the bad ones too.

## The part I would change

Now the detail. The filing does not only say $375 million. It says that this represents a 10 percent annualized return on alternative net investments, that Athene's main pooled vehicle returned an estimated 9 percent annualized, and that its other alternative investments returned an estimated 11 percent annualized.

Those are one quarter's returns multiplied up to a year. The return actually earned in the three months was about a quarter of that: roughly 2.4 percent, by my arithmetic, for the 10 percent figure.

Annualizing is a convention, and the filing labels it clearly. But it is a convention that does a particular job. It turns a quarter into a statement about a year that has not happened, and it does so most persuasively exactly when the asset is one whose returns do not repeat from quarter to quarter. A bond's coupon can fairly be annualized. A quarter of returns on alternative investments is the thing least suited to it.

I would publish the quarter's return as the quarter's return, and give the annualized figure, if at all, as the footnote.

## What it is worth

The objection to early disclosure is that it invites trading on partial information: one line, without the context of the rest. I do not find that convincing. The alternative is not that the information stays unknown. It is that it arrives later, at once, to everybody, in the most crowded hour of the quarter. Partial information honestly labelled is better than complete information delivered in a way nobody can absorb.

The test for Apollo is the quarter in which this line disappoints. If the same notice appears then, four weeks early and with the same plain caveats, the habit is worth what it looks like. I would like to see more companies with a volatile line take it up, and I would like to see all of them drop the multiplication.
